# price_momentum_20bar

**Timestamp:** 2026-03-12 19:19
**Verdict:** REJECTED

## Hypothesis
A simple 20-bar price momentum signal (close-over-close return) captures directional trending behaviour that precedes elevated realised volatility. When price has moved strongly in one direction over the recent 20 bars (~200 seconds), it indicates the market is in an active trending regime with higher subsequent volatility, as participants chase or fade the move. Conversely, near-zero momentum suggests a quiet, mean-reverting regime with lower expected volatility.

## Construction
Compute the percentage return of the closing price over the last 20 bars: (close[t] - close[t-20]) / close[t-20]. Take the absolute value of this return to capture directional magnitude regardless of sign, since both sharp rallies and sharp sell-offs are associated with elevated subsequent volatility. This produces a non-negative scalar per bar representing the strength of recent directional momentum.

## Rejection Criteria
Reject if: (1) out-of-sample RMSE improvement over rolling volatility baseline is zero or negative across both EURGBP and GBPUSD; (2) feature importance drops by more than 40% between any two consecutive walk-forward windows indicating noise-fitting; (3) signal shows no predictive lift in at least two of the four named volatility regimes (pre-COVID calm, COVID crisis, 2022 elevated, 2023 post-normalisation); (4) monotonic degradation in IC or RMSE improvement is observed across chronological test folds suggesting signal decay.

## Summary
The feature shows strong headline RMSE improvements over both baselines across both pairs, and no monotonic decay is detected, which looks promising on the surface. However, importance drift is severely elevated at 69.1% for EURGBP and 89.7% for GBPUSD, both far exceeding the 40% rejection threshold defined in P03 and the pre-stated rejection criteria. This means the model is not consistently relying on this feature to generate its improvements — the signal contribution is unstable and likely noise-driven, even if the aggregate accuracy numbers look good. The aggregate importance figure of 79.4% appears to be a reporting artefact or aggregation error inconsistent with per-pair importance values of 1.2% and 1.46%, which further undermines confidence in the results. Until importance stability is established, the performance improvements cannot be attributed to this feature with any confidence.

## Next Action
Investigate and resolve the discrepancy between the reported aggregate importance of 79.4% and the per-pair importance values of ~1.2-1.5%, then re-run the walk-forward evaluation with importance drift measured at the individual fold level to determine whether the instability is driven by a specific regime or is pervasive before reconsidering the feature for promotion.
